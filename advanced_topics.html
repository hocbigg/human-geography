Human Geography: Advanced Specializations

These advanced specialization tracks build directly on the core undergraduate curriculum in human geography. Each track represents a major subfield or methodological frontier. Self-directed learners should select one or two tracks aligned with their specific intellectual or professional goals rather than attempting to complete all tracks.

  • Critical Geopolitics, Borders & Decolonial Spatialities: Interrogates statecraft, territoriality, border militarization, carceral spaces, and Indigenous counter-cartographies.
  • Political Ecology, Environmental Justice & Climate Futures: Investigates the power dynamics governing resource access, environmental racism, urban metabolism, and climate adaptation.
  • Global Production, Uneven Development & Critical Urbanism: Analyzes the spatial logic of multi-scalar commodity chains, diverse community economies, planetary urbanization, and global logistics.
  • Social Geographies of Difference, Embodiment & Mobilities: Explores how spatial structures co-constitute gender, sexuality, health disparities, and transnational mobilities.
  • Geographic Data Science, Critical GIS & Spatial Humanities: Integrates computational spatial data science and deep mapping with critical analysis of cartographic power and digital surveillance.

Critical Geopolitics, Borders & Decolonial Spatialities

This track examines how political power is spatialized, contested, and legitimized through state borders, penal systems, and colonial territorial architectures.

Critical Geopolitics: Critical Geopolitics: The Politics of Writing Global Space (Gearóid Ó Tuathail) - Foundational text demonstrating how political elites deploy geographic discourse and spatial metaphors to justify state power and military intervention.

Border Regimes & Securitization: Violent Borders: Refugees and the Right to Move (Reece Jones) - Critical examination of how state borders and militarized barriers regulate human mobility, generate systemic violence, and protect uneven wealth distribution.

Carceral & Abolition Geographies: Golden Gulag: Prisons, Surplus, Crisis, and Opposition in Globalizing California (Ruth Wilson Gilmore) - Seminal study analyzing mass incarceration through geographic lenses of surplus land, surplus capital, surplus labor, and state capacity.

Decolonial & Indigenous Spatialities: A Billion Black Anthropocenes or None (Kathryn Yusoff) - Open-access monograph that interrogates the racialized, colonial origins of geology and cartography while rethinking spatial extractivism.

Political Ecology, Environmental Justice & Climate Futures

This track explores the political and economic forces that shape environmental degradation, resource conflict, toxic exposure, and climate vulnerability.

Critical Political Ecology: Political Ecology: A Critical Introduction (Paul Robbins) - The benchmark advanced text exploring nature-society dialectics, conservation politics, environmental degradation, and ecological identity.

Environmental Justice: Environmental Justice (University of Michigan / Coursera) - Free-to-audit course examining the historical origins, legal frameworks, and grassroots activism addressing environmental racism and toxic exposure.

Climate Vulnerability & Spatial Adaptation: Climate Change 2022: Impacts, Adaptation and Vulnerability (IPCC Working Group II) - Authoritative open scientific assessment detailing uneven regional vulnerabilities, adaptation limits, and spatial equity considerations across the globe.

Urban Political Ecology: In the Nature of Cities: Urban Political Ecology and the Politics of Urban Metabolism (Nik Heynen, Maria Kaika, & Erik Swyngedouw, Eds.) - Foundational volume analyzing cities as hybrid socio-natural entities produced by unequal flows of water, energy, waste, and capital.

Global Production, Uneven Development & Critical Urbanism

This track analyzes the spatial organization of the global economy, alternative economic models, logistical systems, and contemporary urban transformations.

Global Production Networks: Global Shift: Mapping the Changing Contours of the World Economy (Peter Dicken) - Comprehensive masterwork tracking the spatial distribution of transnational corporate networks, international division of labor, and state regulatory roles.

Post-Development & Community Economies: Take Back the Economy: An Ethical Guide for Transforming Our Communities (J.K. Gibson-Graham, Jenny Cameron, & Stephen Healy) - Actionable framework reframing economic spaces around shared commons, ethical labor, and diverse community-led economies.

Planetary Urbanization: New Urban Spaces: Urban Theory and the Planetary (Neil Brenner) - Advanced theoretical text rethinking urban studies beyond traditional city boundaries to include logistical networks and operational landscapes.

Transport & Logistical Systems: The Geography of Transport Systems (Jean-Paul Rodrigue) - Comprehensive open-access textbook covering transportation networks, terminal operations, supply-chain logistics, and spatial accessibility.

Social Geographies of Difference, Embodiment & Mobilities

This track investigates how social categories such as gender, race, ability, and health are mutually constituted by spatial arrangements and mobility systems.

Feminist Geographies: Gender, Identity and Place: Understanding Feminist Geographies (Linda McDowell) - Definitive survey examining how patriarchal power and gender roles are reproduced and contested across homes, workplaces, and public spaces.

Urban Inequality & Segregation: Rethink the City: New Approaches to Global and Local Urban Challenges (Delft University of Technology / edX) - Free-to-audit course exploring spatial justice, residential segregation, and equitable housing solutions with a strong emphasis on the Global South.

Geographies of Health & Well-being: Geographies of Health: An Introduction (Anthony C. Gatrell & Susan J. Elliott) - Core advanced text examining the spatial distribution of disease, local environmental health risks, therapeutic landscapes, and healthcare access.

Critical Mobilities: On the Move: Mobility in the Modern Western World (Tim Cresswell) - Conceptual framework exploring how physical movement is imbued with meaning, power, and differential rights in contemporary society.

Geographic Data Science, Critical GIS & Spatial Humanities

This track bridges computational spatial analysis with critical interrogations of algorithmic power, digital surveillance, and non-Cartesian deep mapping.

Geographic Data Science: Geographic Data Science with Python (Sergio J. Rey, Dani Arribas-Bel, & Levi J. Wolf) - Complete open-access interactive computational textbook covering exploratory spatial data analysis, spatial weights, clustering, and spatial regression.

Geographic Data Science: A Course in Geographic Data Science (Dani Arribas-Bel / University of Liverpool) - Complementary open university course offering lecture slides, practical Jupyter notebooks, and applied datasets using the modern Python geospatial stack.

Critical GIS: New Lines: Critical GIS and the Edge of the New Map (Matthew W. Wilson) - Critical monograph evaluating the political implications, surveillance capacities, and counter-mapping alternatives of digital geographic software.

Spatial Humanities: Deep Maps and Spatial Narratives (David J. Bodenhamer, John Corrigan, & Trevor M. Harris, Eds.) - Explores how geographical information systems can integrate qualitative narratives, cultural memory, and historical archives to construct multi-layered representations of place.
