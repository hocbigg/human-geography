Hocbigg - Human Geography

Path to a free self-taught education in Human Geography!

Last updated on: 2026-10-04

Introduction

Human geography investigates how human societies create, organize, and contest space and place, as well as how social processes shape and are shaped by the physical environment. While physical geography examines natural systems like landforms, climate, and hydrology, human geography focuses on the spatial organization of culture, political power, economic networks, settlements, and nature–society relations. Studying human geography equips you to look past surface-level events and analyze the underlying spatial structures — from global supply chains and geopolitical borders to neighborhood segregation and environmental injustice — that govern daily life across the globe.

This curriculum is designed for self-directed, independent learners approaching human geography from the ground up. It assumes no previous academic background in geography, spatial science, or social theory. The material emphasizes conceptual clarity, critical spatial reasoning, and practical spatial literacy over formal academic research protocols, making it suitable for career-changers, polymaths, and curious generalists seeking to understand how the modern world is organized.

How to Navigate the Curriculum

The curriculum is structured into twelve core subjects that logically build from foundational theory to applied thematic analysis:

  • Start with the foundations and spatial tools: Begin with Introduction to Human Geography, Core Spatial Concepts, and Geographic Thought and Theory. These subjects establish the essential conceptual vocabulary — such as space, place, scale, landscape, and territoriality — and trace the discipline's intellectual evolution. Follow these immediately with Cartography and Map Literacy and Geographic Information Systems (GIS) to develop critical map-reading skills and a working understanding of digital spatial data.
  • Explore the thematic domains: Once you have a firm grasp of spatial concepts and tools, work through the primary subfields: Population and Migration Geography, Cultural Geography, Economic Geography, Political Geography, Urban Geography, and Human–Environment Geography. These represent the major thematic pillars of the discipline. While presented in a logical sequence, they can be explored in whatever order best matches your interests.
  • Synthesize at the global scale: Conclude with World Regional Geography, which draws together the cultural, political, economic, and environmental frameworks from earlier subjects to examine how these forces play out across the Earth's distinct continental realms.

Next Steps

This document covers only the universal, undergraduate-level core that every student of human geography needs before specializing. It avoids narrow elective subfields and advanced quantitative modeling to maintain a focused, achievable learning path.

Once you have completed this foundational core, you can pursue specialized tracks in Advanced Topics (such as critical geopolitics, political ecology, or geographic data science), engage directly with field-defining historical texts in Readings, or supplement your study with recorded university lectures in Courses.

Curriculum

Introduction to Human Geography

This subject introduces the spatial perspective, exploring how human societies organize space, interact across places, and understand the interconnected modern world.

Introduction to Human Geography (David Dorrell & Joseph Henderson, Eds.) - A comprehensive, openly accessible peer-reviewed textbook that introduces spatial terminology, population dynamics, cultural patterns, and economic systems.

Human Geography: Places and Regions in Global Context (Paul L. Knox & Sallie A. Marston) - A commercial alternative to Dorrell & Henderson that places greater emphasis on globalization and visual case studies.

Crash Course Geography (CrashCourse / Alizé Carrère) - A complementary high-production video series covering the fundamental principles of physical and human geography; best watched alongside the introductory readings.

Core Spatial Concepts

This subject unpacks the foundational conceptual toolkit of the discipline, examining how geographers define, debate, and apply concepts like space, place, scale, landscape, and territory.

Key Concepts in Geography (Nicholas Clifford, Sarah L. Holloway, Stephen P. Rice, & Gill Valentine, Eds.) - An authoritative collection of essays by leading geographers dissecting the core terminology and philosophical debates underpinning geographical inquiry.

Place: An Introduction (Tim Cresswell) - A focused monograph that serves as an accessible conceptual companion to Clifford et al., tracing how "place" has been theorized across humanistic, cultural, and critical geography.

Geographic Thought and Theory

This subject explores the intellectual history of geography, tracing how the discipline evolved from environmental determinism and regional description to spatial science, Marxist political economy, feminism, and postcolonial theory.

Geographic Thought: A Critical Introduction (Tim Cresswell) - The definitive, highly readable narrative textbook exploring the major intellectual movements and philosophical paradigms that have shaped modern geography.

Cartography and Map Literacy

This subject examines the art, science, and ethics of mapmaking, covering coordinate systems, projections, symbolization, and the ways maps inevitably distort or persuade.

How to Lie with Maps (Mark Monmonier) - A canonical, accessible study of cartographic abstraction, scale, and projection, illustrating how all maps must distort reality to communicate and how those distortions are manipulated.

Cartography (Esri) - A practical, free online course that builds directly on Monmonier’s principles by teaching modern digital map styling, typography, and visual hierarchy using ArcGIS Pro.

Geographic Information Systems (GIS)

This subject covers the fundamental principles of digital geospatial data, including vector and raster data models, coordinate reference systems, spatial databases, and introductory map analysis.

Essentials of Geographic Information Systems (Jonathan Campbell & Michael Shin) - A clear, conceptually grounded open textbook covering spatial data models, coordinate transformations, and spatial analysis techniques without locking the reader into proprietary workflows.

The Nature of Geographic Information (David DiBiase / Penn State OER) - An open courseware text that serves as a thorough conceptual alternative to Campbell & Shin, detailing the geodetic and computational foundations of GIS.

Fundamentals of GIS (University of California, Davis / Coursera) - A structured, audit-free video course that pairs with the theoretical texts by providing hands-on software demonstrations, data management exercises, and map output workflows.

Geographic Information System (GIS) Tutorial (MIT OpenCourseWare) - A self-paced set of tutorial exercises that serves as a fast-track, software-agnostic alternative for practicing basic geoprocessing in either QGIS or ArcGIS Pro.

Population and Migration Geography

This subject analyzes the spatial distribution of human populations, demographic transitions, fertility and mortality patterns, and the socio-spatial drivers of internal and international migration.

Population Geography: Tools and Issues (K. Bruce Newbold) - An issue-driven foundational textbook that introduces demographic measures and spatial models alongside real-world migration, refugee movements, and public health dilemmas.

Cultural Geography

This subject examines how culture is spatially constituted and contested, focusing on language, religion, identity, cultural diffusion, and the production of cultural landscapes.

Cultural Geography: A Critical Introduction (Don Mitchell) - A classic, politically rigorous textbook analyzing culture as an arena of social power, labor struggles, and ideological conflict materialized in the built environment.

Cultural Geography (Mike Crang) - An alternative introductory text to Mitchell, offering a more post-structural and literary approach centered on cultural perception, everyday spaces, and symbolic representation.

Economic Geography

This subject investigates the spatial organization of economic activity, including uneven geographical development, industrial location, global production networks, and regional economic restructuring.

Economic Geography: A Contemporary Introduction (Neil M. Coe, Philip F. Kelly, & Henry W. C. Yeung) - The benchmark undergraduate introduction to economic geography, organizing the global economy around commodity chains, labor regimes, state governance, and local place-based dynamics.

Political Geography

This subject explores the relationships between space, territory, and political power, analyzing the modern state system, sovereignty, border politics, nationalism, and global geopolitics.

Political Geography: World-Economy, Nation-State, and Locality (Colin Flint & Peter J. Taylor) - The definitive textbook applying a world-systems framework to political geography, explaining how global geopolitical orders interact with state structures and local electoral politics.

Urban Geography

This subject examines the processes and spatial forms of urbanization, including central place systems, internal urban models, spatial segregation, gentrification, and global urban networks.

Urban Geography: A Critical Introduction (Andrew E. G. Jonas, Eugene McCann, & Mary Thomas) - A comprehensive critical text that blends traditional spatial models of the city with contemporary debates on neoliberal urbanism, suburban sprawl, and the right to the city.

Human–Environment Geography

This subject explores the recursive relationships between society and the non-human world, focusing on political ecology, resource management, environmental hazards, and sustainability debates.

Environment and Society: A Critical Introduction (Paul Robbins, John G. Hintz, & Sarah A. Moore) - A foundational textbook that equips learners with conceptual frameworks (including political economy, environmental ethics, and social construction of nature) and applies them to everyday ecological commodities.

GEOG 30N: Environment and Society in a Changing World (Penn State OER) - A freely accessible, open courseware curriculum that complements Robbins et al. by exploring coupled human-environment systems, vulnerability, and environmental governance through structured modules.

World Regional Geography

This culminating core subject applies the thematic subfields of human geography across the Earth's distinct macro-realms, synthesizing environmental, cultural, political, and economic patterns.

World Regional Geography: People, Places and Globalization (Royal Berglee) - A comprehensive open-access textbook structured region by region, demonstrating how global forces and local geographies intersect across the world's major continental realms.
